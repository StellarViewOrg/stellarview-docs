An asset on Stellar is identified by a code, such as `USDC`, plus the account that issued it. XLM is the native asset and has no issuer.

## Discovering assets

The **Assets** page lists the assets on the current network. Each entry shows:

- **Code and issuer**, the asset's identity.
- **Home domain**, read from the issuer account.
- **Statistics:** holders, trustlines, and trade volume.

## Asset details

- **Metadata** from the issuer's `stellar.toml`: name, description, logo, and compliance information.
- **Holders**, the accounts holding the asset.
- **Trades**, recent trades involving it.
- **Pools**, every trading pair and liquidity pool the asset is part of.

## About `stellar.toml`

Issuers publish metadata in a `stellar.toml` file on their home domain. The explorer fetches it through a server-side proxy that only accepts HTTPS and rejects localhost and private address ranges, so a malicious home domain can't use the explorer to reach internal networks.

<Note>
  Anyone can issue an asset with any code. The issuer and its home domain are what tell you whether a `USDC` is the one you expect.
</Note>
